PIPVIS: Explaining AI Predictions of Pedestrian Behaviour
Explainable AI · Computer vision data · Dashboard

![PIPVIS hero image](images\projects\PIPVIS\PIPVIS-MAIN-banner.png)

Other images:
![PIPVIS Depth Semantic Full image](images\projects\PIPVIS\IMG-1-depth-sem-full.png)
![PIPVIS Pose Semantic False Positive image](images\projects\PIPVIS\IMG-2-pose-semantic-FP.png)
![PIPVIS Model Diagnosis Treemap Hover image](images\projects\PIPVIS\IMG-3-TP-stat-hover.png)

Autonomous vehicles rely on AI to predict whether a pedestrian will cross, but these models are hard to debug. With SYSTRA UK & Ireland and transport AI researchers, I built an interactive dashboard that explains a model’s decisions using six hours of dashcam footage (about 300,000 labelled frames, 1,842 pedestrians). The model scored 91.4% on validation data, and the dashboard showed why the rest failed: in several missed crossings, the pose estimator had locked onto a nearby occluding pedestrian. The team now has a concrete fix to test.

Sustainable Food Sales Explorer: Seasonal Footprint of UK Food Purchasing
Data engineering · Sustainability analytics · Dashboard

![SFSE hero image](images\projects\SFSE\SFSE-MAIN-banner.png)

Other images:
![SFSE GHGE Timeline image](images\projects\SFSE\IMG-1-GHGE-timeline.png)
![SFSE Per-Kg Heatmap image](images\projects\SFSE\IMG-2-Per-Kg-heatmap.png)
![SFSE Land Use Timeline image](images\projects\SFSE\IMG-3-LU-timeline.png)

Every shopping basket carries a carbon, land and water footprint, but how that changes through the year had never been measured at this level of detail. I built a Python pipeline linking 160 million product sales from a major UK retailer to environmental impact data, and a D3.js dashboard that lets researchers explore weekly footprints across up to 65 food categories, with overlays for holidays, cultural events, school terms, heatwaves and sporting events. Our festive analysis found emissions from food sales rose 15.3% in December, with 6.6% of the rise in per-kilogram terms coming from what people chose, not just how much they bought.
